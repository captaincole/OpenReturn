# bran — Form 7207, an energy components plant

A made-up taxpayer. Every name and amount is invented. Synthetic and
**unverified**: no answer key stands behind the expected values. They are the
engine's output, checked by hand line by line (below), then locked.

## Who

Bran Tolliver, single, a full-year Texas resident (no state return). His
income is his plant, Tolliver Energy Works (Schedule C, material
participation): gross receipts 6,400,000, expenses 5,200,000, net profit
1,200,000. The plant is a small business taxpayer that keeps no inventory and
treats its materials as supplies (line 22), as the `aram` scenario does.

In 2025 the plant produced and sold, to unrelated buyers, a component of
every Part II row except line 6b: solar cells, wafers, polysilicon,
backsheet, modules, torque tubes and fasteners; small wind blades, nacelles
and towers, two offshore foundations and two offshore wind vessels; six kinds
of inverter; electrode active materials; battery cells and both kinds of
module; and three critical minerals. He sold $50,000 of the credit to an
unrelated buyer under section 6418.

## What it covers

One Form 7207 with Parts I to IV. Line 8a goes to Form 3800 Part III line 1b,
column (e); the transfer is column (f). The credit is more than Form 3800
allows (the tentative minimum tax is the limit), so line 38 is the limit and
the rest carries forward. Schedule 3 line 6a and Form 1040 line 20 take line
38. Form 6251 is listed because Form 3800 line 14 reads its line 9.

## Documents

None. The sales are his answers from the plant's production and sales
records; the transfer from the transfer agreement. No IRS form reports them.
